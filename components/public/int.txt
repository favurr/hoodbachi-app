Hibachi Lou — Visual Direction

Overall feel
Modern food brand × editorial magazine × Pittsburgh street culture.
Not:
dark luxury restaurant
generic food-truck template
overly polished SaaS design
excessive glassmorphism
Instead:
Cream backgrounds. Loud red. Warm yellow. Huge typography. Beautiful food photography. Lots of whitespace. Strong editorial layouts.

The reference's use of a dominant red/yellow/cream palette is exactly the kind of energy I'd carry over, but make it more sophisticated for Hibachi Lou.

01 — Page Loader
Don't make it a generic spinner.
The loader should feel like part of the brand.
Concept
Full-screen cream background.
Centered:
HIBACHI LOU
in The Seasons.
Underneath:
PITTSBURGH / 412
in Geist Mono.
Then a loading indicator.
Something like:
HIBACHI LOU
[████████████████░░░░]
412 / PITTSBURGH
The progress number uses Geist Mono and should be positioned at the bottom right corner of the loader and BIG.

Animation
The page starts with:
0%
and progresses to:
100%
Meanwhile the logo subtly scales from:
0.96 → 1
Then:
loader background slides upward

hero content is revealed underneath
navigation fades in
hero image finishes its entrance
No 3-second artificial loading screen.
If the page is already ready at 400ms, get out of the way.
The loader should communicate brand, not waste someone's time.

Animation Language

The animations should be editorial, not "look what GSAP can do."

Typography

Headings reveal upward:

translateY(100%)
→
translateY(0)

with a clipped container.

Images
scale(1.08)
→
scale(1)
Cards

Small movement:

y: 20px → 0
opacity: 0 → 1
Marquee

Continuous horizontal movement.

Menu

Horizontal scrolling gallery on desktop.

Truck

Hero truck can have a tiny entrance movement:

x: 80px → 0
opacity: 0 → 1
Page transitions

Cream → cream.

No dramatic black transition.

The Design Principle

The reference you gave me has something I like a lot:

It isn't afraid of color.

That's what I want to preserve.

But I'd make Hibachi Lou considerably more contemporary.

Think:

The Seasons + Geist + cream paper + aggressive red + yellow accents + huge food photography + mono metadata + editorial grid.

That combination could look very, very good.

And the page loader becomes the first piece of that experience rather than an afterthought.
